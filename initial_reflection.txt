1. Story Overview
Premchand's ???? explores poverty, hunger, social marginalisation and human behaviour through the lives of Ghisu and Madhav. The story also presents the suffering of Budhiya, Madhav's pregnant wife, whose neglect and death expose the gender inequalities within their social and economic circumstances.
2. Initial Reading and Observations
The initial reading of selected passages from the beginning, middle, ending and random sections of the story suggests that poverty and individual behaviour are closely connected.
Ghisu and Madhav appear unwilling to work and frequently blame others for their circumstances. However, their extreme poverty, hunger and social marginalisation also need to be considered while understanding their actions.
The episode in which both men continue roasting potatoes while Budhiya suffers from labour pain is particularly disturbing. Their fear that someone might take away the potatoes reveals their material insecurity, while their neglect of Budhiya exposes their lack of care and responsibility towards her.
Their decision to spend the money collected for Budhiya's funeral on food and alcohol further raises questions about morality, survival and human sensitivity.
3. Personal Reflection
I initially viewed Ghisu and Madhav as irresponsible and lazy individuals. However, a closer reading made me consider how extreme poverty, hunger and social exclusion might influence their behaviour.
The story also made me question why Budhiya's suffering receives so little attention from the two men. Her experience reveals how poverty and gender inequality can intersect, making women particularly vulnerable.
I therefore want to examine the characters without simply justifying or condemning their actions.

6. Researcher Positionality and Reflexivity
As a researcher, I approach the story with an interest in understanding poverty, social inequality and human behaviour. My initial response to Ghisu and Madhav was critical because of their apparent irresponsibility and neglect of Budhiya.
However, I recognise that judging their actions without examining their material and social conditions could lead to a limited interpretation. My reading is also shaped by my contemporary understanding of gender, caste and economic inequality.
I will therefore remain reflexive and examine both individual responsibility and structural conditions while interpreting the story.
